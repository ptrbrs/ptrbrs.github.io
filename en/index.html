Design director and multidisciplinary designer with over 25 years of experience working across apparel, branding and visual systems for international brands.

About.Me

Early years

It would be inaccurate to claim that his current professional path was a childhood ambition. At the time, there was no particular interest — only an early, latent aptitude for it.

He was a distinctly average child. By kindergarten, he had already abandoned the habit of eating everything indiscriminately — Legos and crystal balls included. Upon entering school, the familiar feeling of “I hate everything” could easily have taken hold. His parents, however, intervened early and redirected his excess energy toward sports, hoping to make him at least somewhat more manageable by their standards.

There was real potential there. He could have become a talented athlete. But what began as raw exploitation rather than conscious development inevitably led to recurring injuries, making a professional sports career unsustainable.
That realisation — coupled with disappointment — forced a change in direction and opened the door to alternative paths of self-realisation.

Skateboarding might have been a better long-term option than running, but graffiti in public spaces proved far more compelling. Time was spent close to surfaces — legal or otherwise, it hardly mattered. Still, one thing was clear: “This can’t last forever.” A step beyond was inevitable.

Pro-years

His entry into the world of advertising was entirely accidental — but once inside, it quickly became an integral part of his life. It evolved into work, profession, and occasionally even recreation. That said, it was never the only thing he did. In that environment, turning such a path into a lifelong career was more an ideal than a realistic expectation.

The first position at an advertising company felt closer to factory work than creative exploration — monumental, repetitive tasks defined the daily routine. Still, the experience proved valuable. It built a solid understanding of software, workflows, and production processes. What it lacked was room for genuine creative expression, which eventually made the search for new challenges unavoidable.

A move to a smaller company followed, where progression to senior designer came quickly. For the first time, there was space to operate freely and apply ideas without constant constraint. Brand elements, custom graphics, multimedia presentations, vehicle wraps — nearly everything within the scope of creative design passed through his hands. Then came the turn of the millennium, and with it, a fundamental shift.

An invitation arrived from the founder of the motorcycle apparel brand MUGENRACE. The collaboration extended beyond borders and cultures, bringing a new level of complexity. Responsibilities expanded to include visual identity development, apparel design, and oversight of the company’s complete visual presence. Creative capabilities aligned well with the role, but it was the personal growth — shaped by international exposure and cultural diversity — that ultimately completed the picture.

The outcome required little explanation. Over nearly two decades, each challenge was met with focus and adaptability. Eventually, a natural transition point emerged: the moment to pursue new goals and opportunities. Graphic design and visual thinking remain core elements of his professional identity, but attention has since shifted toward ideas that demand the same balance of creativity, structure, and problem-solving. Experience, discipline, and a distinctly individual perspective continue to define whatever comes next.

p.s.:

He is not inclined to make noise purely for attention. Clarity, purpose, and letting the quality of the work speak are preferred over volume. Every task is approached with intention and structure, with a clear focus on delivering value through thoughtful execution. Rather than relying on self-promotion, the emphasis remains on showing — not telling. This is why the "works" collection exists: a deliberately curated selection that reflects how he thinks, solves problems, and creates.
